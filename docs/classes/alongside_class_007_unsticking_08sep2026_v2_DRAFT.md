## What this class is for, and the problem it has

🔴 **This is the class most at risk of being a worse version of a session.** Standing Up had the same risk and answered it by naming what the movements are *for*. That answer will not work twice — and here there is a harder problem underneath it.

**Stiffness is the one thing people expect an immediate result from.** You stretch, you feel looser, you conclude it worked. Fifteen minutes of hip work does produce that feeling. **And it is almost entirely temporary** — range that comes back within the hour, which is why people have been stretching their hips for years and are exactly as stiff as they were.

So this class has to do the thing the other five do, but pointed at the one place a person will actively resist it:

> **You will feel looser at the end of this. That feeling is real and it will not last. The part that lasts is doing it again in the next class.**

⚫ **It says this at the start, not the end.** Said at the end, after they already feel better, it reads as the class talking itself down. Said at the start it is a frame — and it turns the temporary feeling from evidence of success into something they were told to expect.

## The script

`[SCREEN]` is what appears; `[VOICE]` is what is said. `⏸` is silence, held.

---

### 1 · Before we start (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Hips. Fifteen minutes.**
You'll feel looser afterwards. That part doesn't last — the doing it again does.

`[VOICE]` This one's hips. Floor if you can get there, chair the whole way through if not — and the chair version isn't a reduced one, it's the same class sitting down.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Something I'd rather say now than at the end. You'll probably feel looser when we finish. That feeling is real, and it mostly wears off within the hour.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` That's not me being gloomy. It's the reason people stretch their hips for years and stay exactly as stiff as they were — they were measuring the wrong thing. **The bit that changes anything is doing it again in the next class.**

`⏸ 25s`

🔴 **The whole class turns on this being said first.** It is the same refusal the other five make — *not a today question*, *on a staircase in a month* — but here it arrives **before** the person feels the pleasant thing, because afterwards it would sound like an excuse.

---

### 2 · Hip circles (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Hip circles** — standing, holding something. Slow, and small to start.
Let them get bigger only if they want to.

`[VOICE]` Stand and hold the back of a chair. One knee up, and draw a circle with it. Slowly.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Start small. Let it get bigger only if it wants to — you're not opening anything, you're asking.

`⏸ 35s`

`[VOICE]` Other side when you're ready.

`⏸ 35s`

**Exercise:** `hip-circles-standing` · **sit-out:** yes · **seated route:** `seated-figure-4-stretch` · **stop cue:** only as big as it wants to go

---

### 3 · Figure four (2 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Figure four** — on your back, or seated.
Where it's a stretch, not where it's a fight.

`[VOICE]` On your back, knees bent. Cross one ankle over the opposite knee, and reach through to pull the back leg towards you. Or do exactly the same sitting in a chair — cross the ankle over and lean forward a little.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` Find where it's a stretch. Not where it's a fight. There's a version of this that's just gritting your teeth, and it doesn't do anything the gentle one doesn't.

`⏸ 45s`

`[VOICE]` Other side.

`⏸ 40s`

**Exercise:** `seated-figure-4-stretch` · **sit-out:** yes · **stop cue:** where it's a stretch, not a fight

---

### 4 · What's different (1 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Anything looser?**
Probably. Remember what we said about that.

`[VOICE]` Anything feel different? There probably is — this is the point in a hip class where things start to give a bit.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` Enjoy it. Just don't file it as proof. It's this afternoon that has an opinion, not right now.

`⏸ 20s`

⚫ **A `reflect` beat that lets the person have the good feeling and declines to let it be the evidence.** Ground asks *is anything different* and accepts "no"; this one asks the same question expecting "yes" and does something different with the answer. **Same beat kind, opposite job.**

---

### 5 · 90-90 (2 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **90-90** — both knees bent, both on the floor, sat between them.
Or the seated version. Turn slowly.

`[VOICE]` Sit on the floor with both knees bent, one leg in front and one out to the side, both shins roughly at right angles. If that's not available, stay in the chair and turn your knees to one side.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` Lean forward over the front leg a little. Only a little.

`⏸ 45s`

`[VOICE]` Come up, and swap sides. Going slowly through the swap is part of it — that transition is the bit your hips will complain about in about six years if nobody asks them to do it.

`⏸ 45s`

**Exercise:** `hip-90-90-stretch` · **sit-out:** yes · **seated route:** `seated-lumbar-rotation` · **stop cue:** only a little

---

### 6 · Hip flexor (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Hip flexor** — half-kneeling, or standing at the chair.
The front of the hip, not the back.

`[VOICE]` Half-kneeling — one knee down, other foot in front. A cushion under the knee if that's kinder. Or stand and take one leg back behind you, holding the chair.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` Tuck your hips underneath you slightly, and you should feel the front of the back leg's hip. That's the bit that spends all day shortened in a chair.

`⏸ 40s`

`[VOICE]` Other side.

`⏸ 30s`

**Exercise:** `hip-flexor-stretch` · **sit-out:** yes · **seated route:** `hip-flexor-progressive` · **stop cue:** where it's a stretch, not a fight

---

### 7 · Settling (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Done.**
The next one is the one that counts.

`[VOICE]` Lie down, or sit back. Nothing to do.

`⏸ 40s`

`[VOICE]` Five bits of hip, none of them heroic.

`⏸ 40s`

`[VOICE]` And the thing from the start still stands. What you're feeling now is today's version. **The next one is the one that counts.**

`⏸ 30s`

**Lighter variant line:** *Three bits of hip, none of them heroic.*

---

### 8 · Leaving (1 min)

`[SCREEN]` **See you in the next class.**

`[VOICE]` That's it. See you in the next class.

`⏸ 15s`

⚫ **The only class whose closing points at the next one.** Every other ends *see you next time*; this ends *see you in the next class*, because repetition is the whole mechanism here and the ending should say so.

🔴 **It said "See you Thursday" in v1.** Graeme: *"This seems like an own goal."* He was right — it assumed a schedule nobody had agreed to, and for anybody who then did not do it on Thursday it was **a small failure the class had handed them**, in a class already asking them to accept that today's good feeling does not count. Naming the next class instead keeps the argument and drops the presumption.

## The lighter variant

**Omits sections 3 and 5** — figure four and 90-90. Ten minutes.

What is left is the circles, the noticing, the hip flexor, the settling and the leaving.

⚫ **The two that come out are the two that need the floor properly.** Circles are standing, the hip flexor works half-kneeling or standing, and both remaining movements have seated routes. **On a bad day the class can be done without getting down onto the floor at all** — which is the same shape as Standing Up's lighter variant, and carries the same worry: it doubles as the version for somebody who cannot get to the floor on any day.

Section 7's line changes: *five bits of hip* is not true of three.

---

## What this class did to the contract

**Nothing broke.** Fifth in a row, on a class deliberately picked as the one most likely to be a session in disguise.

**Every movement has a seated route** — the first class where that is true of all of them, and it is why `seatedRoute: true` sits at class level as well. The two facts are different and both are needed: the class as a whole can be done seated, *and* each individual movement has a named alternative.

⚫ **One thing worth watching.** This class uses `stop cue` twice with the same words — *where it's a stretch, not a fight*. That is deliberate repetition inside one class, and the contract has no opinion about it. **Repeating a cue is probably good** — it is how a phrase becomes a thing the person carries out of the class — but nothing currently distinguishes deliberate repetition from copy-paste.

## Open questions for Graeme

1. ✅ **~~"See you Thursday"~~ — ANSWERED.** Graeme: *"This seems like an own goal. Perhaps the best thing to do is actually say see you in the next class."* Changed in all three places it appeared — the framing at the start, the settling line, and the closing. **All three had to move together or the argument breaks**: the class's whole case is that the next one is what counts, so the phrase carrying that case appears throughout rather than only at the end.

2. **Is "Unsticking" a word you want?** It is plain and slightly wry and matches "Bending". It also implies something is stuck, which is precisely what the person believes and possibly not what you want reinforced on a card.

3. **The honesty at the start may be too much for a first class.** Somebody arriving at Alongside stiff, on day one, is told within ninety seconds that the thing they came for wears off. **That is true, and it may be the wrong first thing to hear** — it might belong from session two onwards, which the contract has no way to express.
