# PART 1: THE COACH IDENTITY

## Who Is This Coach?

The Coach is not a character or persona - it's a consistent presence that adapts its tone while maintaining core values.

### The Coach IS:
- A knowledgeable professional who genuinely cares
- Someone who's seen it all and doesn't judge
- Patient, but not passive
- Warm, but not saccharine
- Direct, but not harsh
- Someone who believes in you more than you believe in yourself
- A guide, not a boss

### The Coach IS NOT:
- A drill sergeant ("No excuses!")
- A disappointed parent ("You should have...")
- A peppy influencer ("You've GOT this babe!")
- A detached algorithm ("Based on your data...")
- An overfamiliar friend ("Hey buddy!")
- A therapist (we support wellbeing, not treat conditions)

### The Coach Believes:
- Showing up matters more than performing
- Rest is productive, not lazy
- Your body is wise - listen to it
- Progress isn't linear, and that's okay
- You're not broken, you're adapting
- Every small action compounds over time
- Bad days don't erase good days
- The goal is sustainability, not intensity

# PART 2: LANGUAGE PRINCIPLES

## Words We Use

| Instead of... | We say... |
|---------------|-----------|
| Workout | Session, movement, practice |
| You should | You might, you could, consider |
| You need to | This helps because... |
| Don't forget | When you're ready |
| You missed | Welcome back |
| You failed | That's feedback |
| Push through | Listen to your body |
| No pain no gain | Discomfort is information |
| Cheat day | Rest day, recovery, life |
| Be consistent | Show up when you can |
| You're behind | You're exactly where you are |
| At least you... | You did... |
| Just try to... | You could... |
| You only did... | You did... |

## Words We NEVER Use

These words are banned from all Coach scripts:

- **Lazy** - Ever, for any reason
- **Excuse** - Their reasons are valid
- **Should have** - No retrospective guilt
- **At least** - Minimises their effort
- **Just** (diminishing) - "Just 5 minutes" diminishes the action
- **But** after praise - "Great work, but..." negates the praise
- **Streak** - Implies breakable chain
- **Perfect** - Unattainable standard
- **Behind schedule** - Schedule serves them, not vice versa
- **Make up for** - No debt to repay
- **Catch up** - No falling behind
- **Guilty pleasure** - No guilt
- **Earning** food - No transactional eating
- **Burning off** - No punishment framing

## Tone Principles

1. **Assume good faith** - They're doing their best with what they have
2. **State, don't ask** - "Here's what might help" not "Do you want to try...?"
3. **Explain why** - Never just tell them what to do
4. **Normalise struggle** - "This is hard for most people" removes shame
5. **Offer options** - "You could do X, or if that doesn't feel right, Y"
6. **End on agency** - They decide, always
7. **Be specific** - "Your hip flexors" not "your body"
8. **Acknowledge reality** - Don't pretend bad days are good

## Framing Principles

### Reframe Negatives as Neutrals

| User feels... | Coach reframes as... |
|---------------|----------------------|
| "I failed" | "You got information" |
| "I'm weak" | "You're building" |
| "I can't do it" | "Not yet, or not today" |
| "I skipped" | "You rested" |
| "I'm inconsistent" | "Life is variable" |
| "I'm behind" | "You're where you are" |

### Celebrate Process, Not Just Outcomes

| Instead of... | Say... |
|---------------|--------|
| "You completed all exercises!" | "You showed up and moved." |
| "You hit your target!" | "You're building a practice." |
| "Perfect session!" | "That was solid work." |

# PART 3: PERSONALITY VARIANTS

## Overview

| Personality | Core Tone | Energy Level | Word Density | Best For |
|-------------|-----------|--------------|--------------|----------|
| **Steady** | Calm, grounded, reassuring | Medium | Medium | Default - anxiety, perfectionism, injury recovery |
| **Energetic** | Upbeat, motivating, warm | High | Higher | Low-activation ADHD, needs external momentum |
| **Minimal** | Direct, efficient, clear | Low | Sparse | Experienced users, sensory sensitivity, prefers less |
| **Nurturing** | Gentle, emotionally attuned, soft | Low-medium | Medium-high | Trauma history, chronic illness, high self-criticism |

## Personality Deep Dives

### Steady (Default)

**Voice direction:** "Speak like a trusted physiotherapist who's seen hundreds of patients. Calm confidence. Nothing surprises you, nothing disappoints you. You know this works because you've seen it work."

**Characteristics:**
- Measured pace
- Grounded tone
- Explains the why
- Acknowledges difficulty without drama
- Quietly confident

**Example greeting:** "Good to see you. Let's see how you're feeling today."

**Example encouragement:** "That's solid work. You're building something here."

**Example after struggle:** "That was hard. It's supposed to be hard sometimes. You did it anyway."

---

### Energetic

**Voice direction:** "Warm enthusiasm without being performative. You're genuinely excited to help, not putting on a show. Think encouraging older sibling, not fitness influencer."

**Characteristics:**
- Brighter tone
- More words
- Celebrates effort openly
- Uses more affirming language
- Creates momentum

**Example greeting:** "Hey! Great to see you. Ready to move?"

**Example encouragement:** "Yes! That's exactly it. You're getting stronger every time."

**Example after struggle:** "That was tough, and you pushed through. That takes guts. Really well done."

---

### Minimal

**Voice direction:** "Clear, concise, respectful of their time and attention. No fluff, no unnecessary words. Trust them to know what they need."

**Characteristics:**
- Short sentences
- Few words
- Facts over feelings
- Respects autonomy
- Efficient

**Example greeting:** "Ready when you are."

**Example encouragement:** "Done. Good work."

**Example after struggle:** "Hard one. You finished it."

---

### Nurturing

**Voice direction:** "Extra gentle, like talking to someone who's had a hard time and needs to know they're safe. Soft, slow, very attuned to emotional state."

**Characteristics:**
- Softer tone
- More emotional attunement
- Extra validation
- Slower pace
- More check-ins

**Example greeting:** "Hello. I'm glad you're here. How are you feeling today - really?"

**Example encouragement:** "You did that so well. I hope you can feel proud of yourself."

**Example after struggle:** "That was really hard, wasn't it? I want you to know that struggling doesn't mean failing. You showed up when it was difficult. That matters."

# PART 5: EXERCISE VOICE SCRIPTS

## Exercise Voice Principles

The Exercise Voice is different from the Coach Voice:

| Coach Voice | Exercise Voice |
|-------------|----------------|
| Personal, warm | Neutral, functional |
| Explains why | Explains what |
| Emotional connection | Clear instruction |
| Varies by personality | Same for everyone |
| Before/after exercises | During exercises |

**Exercise Voice direction:** "Clear, calm, professional. Like a good yoga instructor - not overly warm, not cold. Just precise and helpful. Slightly slower than conversation pace. Room to breathe."

## Exercise Voice Script Structure

Each exercise has:
1. **Setup** - How to get into position
2. **Execution** - What to do
3. **Cues** - Reminders during the exercise
4. **Transitions** - Switching sides, sets, rest
5. **Completion** - Exercise done

## Example: Hip Flexor Stretch

### Setup
> "Kneeling hip flexor stretch.
>
> Kneel on your right knee. Use a cushion if you need one. Left foot forward, knee over ankle.
>
> Tuck your pelvis under. Imagine bringing your belt buckle up toward your chin."

### Execution
> "Gently shift your weight forward until you feel a stretch in the front of your right hip.
>
> Keep your pelvis tucked. Don't arch your back.
>
> Hold here."

### Cues (during hold)
> [At 15 seconds] "Breathe. Let the stretch deepen naturally."
>
> [At 30 seconds] "Check your pelvis - keep it tucked."

### Transition

# PART 8: COACH RESPONSE TO SPECIFIC CONDITIONS

## Herniated Disc User

**When disc condition is active:**
> "I'm being extra careful with your back today. All exercises are chosen to strengthen without compressing your spine. If anything feels wrong - even a little - stop and tell me."

**When suggesting core work:**
> "This core exercise is specifically good for disc issues. It strengthens the muscles that support your spine without putting load through the disc itself."

## Hamstring Injury User

**When hamstring is recovering:**
> "Your hamstring is still healing, so we're working around it. The glute and hip work we're doing actually helps it recover faster by improving blood flow to the area."

**When suggesting progression:**
> "Your hamstring is getting stronger. I think you're ready for a bit more load on it - but we'll take it slow. Tell me if anything doesn't feel right."

## Menstrual Cycle Phases

**Menstruation (Days 1-5):**
> "Your cycle suggests energy might be lower today. We're keeping it gentle - your body is already working hard."

**Follicular (Days 6-14):**
> "Energy should be building through this phase. Good time for strength work if you're feeling it."

**Ovulation (Days 15-17):**
> "Peak energy window. If you want to push a bit harder today, your body can handle it."

**Luteal (Days 18-28):**
> "Energy typically dips in this phase. Steady, sustainable movement is ideal."

# PART 9: VOICE RECORDING GUIDANCE

## General Direction

- **Pace:** Slightly slower than normal conversation
- **Tone:** Warm but grounded (varies by personality)
- **Breath:** Comfortable pauses, not rushed
- **Energy:** Engaged but not performative

## Recording Tips

1. Record in short segments (one script moment at a time)
2. Leave 0.5s silence at start and end
3. Consistent distance from microphone
4. Consistent environment (no background variation)
5. Record multiple takes, choose best

## File Naming Convention

```
{personality}_{moment}_{variant}.mp3

Examples:
steady_welcome.mp3
```
