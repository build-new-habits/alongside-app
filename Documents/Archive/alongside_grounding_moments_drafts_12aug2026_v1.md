## The structure your two examples gave

You described the plank in three steps and the run in one long sweep, but they are the same shape underneath. That shape is the finding, and it is worth naming before the content:

| Step | Plank | Running |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Contact** | Where do your arms, elbows, forearms, hands meet the ground — where is it most grounding? | The sound of your feet on the ground, your breathing |
| **2. Place** | Imagine a field. Can you feel grass under your hands? | The air on your skin, the cold or the heat, birds, wind |
| **3. Beyond** | Holding this line, part of nature, grass and flowers and meadow growing around you | Someone coming home from work — wondering what their day might have been like |

**Contact → Place → Beyond.** Body, then world, then other people.

**That is the empathy transfer arc, compressed into ninety seconds.** Stage 1 self-recognition, stage 3 situational empathy — the same journey the prompt library walks over eighty-five sessions, happening inside a single plank. The running example ends on *"wondering what their day might have been like"*, which is a stage 3 prompt almost word for word.

If that holds, grounding moments are not a separate feature sitting near empathy transfer. **They are its practice ground** — and the reason it might land for someone who never reaches session eighty-five.

**Implication worth a decision:** one grounding moment should not do all three steps. Step 3 lands on someone in their first week as instruction; on someone in month four it lands as invitation. So the same exercise wants a shallower and a deeper version, selected the way empathy prompts are.

---

## What the plank example also claims

You said mindful practice while exercising produces more muscle definition. That is real — it is usually called attentional focus or mind-muscle connection, and there is EMG evidence for increased activation under internal focus, particularly for isolation work.

**I have not written it into any of the copy below**, for two reasons. It would need a proper citation to sit alongside Plumbly, Zhang and Batson rather than a claim in the coach's mouth. And a grounding moment that promises a physical return stops being grounding and becomes another performance instruction — which is the thing these exist to interrupt.

Worth a note somewhere in Philosophy. Not in the coach's voice mid-plank.

# The fifteen

Tagged by the shape of the movement, not by exercise name, so 550+ exercises can be covered by a few dozen moments rather than hand-writing each.

---

## Isometric holds — plank, wall sit, dead hang, side plank

**G1 · Contact**
> Before the shake starts — where are you actually touching the ground? Forearms, elbows, the heel of your hands. Find the spot taking the most. That one point is doing a lot of quiet work.

**G2 · Place**
> If you were holding this in a field, what would be under your hands? See if you can put grass there. Cool, slightly damp, giving a little.

**G3 · Beyond**
> You are a line held above the ground. Everything around you is growing — grass, flowers, whatever is in your meadow. You are not separate from that. You are part of it, holding still for a moment while it carries on.

---

## Outdoor cardio — run, walk, cycle

**G4 · Contact**
> Listen to your feet. Not the pace of them — the sound. Then your breathing underneath it. Two rhythms, not quite matching.

**G5 · Place**
> What is the air doing to your skin right now? Cold on your face, warm on your arms, moving or still. You are outside and your body already knows it.

**G6 · Place**
> See how far you can hear. Birds, wind, a road somewhere. Something further away than you first noticed.

**G7 · Beyond**
> Someone is walking home ahead of you. You will not know what kind of day they have had. See what happens if you assume it was a hard one.

**G8 · Beyond**
> Somebody is out in their garden. Somebody else has just got in from work. All of that is going on around you while you move through it, and none of it is about you. That is quite a restful thought.

---

## Loaded strength — squat, deadlift, press, row

**G9 · Contact**
> Feel your feet. All of it — heels, the outside edge, the ball of each foot. The floor is pushing back exactly as hard as you are pushing down.

**G10 · Place**
> Whatever is under you now, there is ground under that. Same ground either way, holding you up without being asked.

---

## Floor work — stretches, mobility, mat exercises

**G11 · Contact**
> Let something go heavy. Whatever part of you is touching the floor, stop holding it up. The floor has it.

**G12 · Place**
> Warm ground after a day of sun. That is what you are lying on. Give it a second and see if your body half believes it.

---

## Seated work

**G13 · Contact**
> Notice where the chair is holding you. Backs of the legs, base of the spine. You are supported, right now, without doing anything about it.

---

## Balance work

**G14 · Contact**
> The small corrections you are making — those tiny adjustments at your ankle. Nobody taught you those. Your body is doing arithmetic you never learn consciously.

---

## Indoor cardio machines — treadmill, bike, rower

**G15 · Beyond**
> Nothing outside to look at here. So: think of one person who is having a harder week than you. Not to compare. Just to hold them in mind while you do this.

# Notes on the drafts

**Voice.** Second person, short sentences, no exclamation. Invitations — *see if*, *notice*, *find* — never *you should*. Nothing is checked, scored or asked about afterwards.

**P4 held throughout.** Nothing praises, compares, or attaches a verdict. G14 comes nearest to a compliment and is deliberately about the body's own competence rather than the person's effort — no "well done", no "you're doing great".

**Copy rule 10.1 held.** No internal terms. *Contact*, *Place*, *Beyond*, *grounding moment* are all ours. None appears on screen.

**No time-stamped horizons**, per the locked principle.

**G7 and G8 are the ones to look at hardest.** They are the closest to the running example and the closest to overreach — instructing someone to feel compassion is the fastest way to stop them feeling any. G7 in particular sits right on the line. It may need to be reachable only after some time in the product, or cut.

**G15 is the weakest.** A treadmill offers no world to notice, so it reaches for the empathy move without the sensory step underneath it, and lands more abruptly than the others. Probably needs rewriting rather than keeping.

# Open questions

1. **Do the three steps deepen with time, as suggested above?** If yes, selection reuses the empathy matcher and this is cheap. If no, they are a flat pool and simpler still.
2. **Where does one appear?** During a hold, on the exercise card, or in the rest gap between sets. The plank example implies during.
3. **How often?** Every session is too often — these depend on being slightly unexpected. Empathy transfer uses one every four sessions; something similar seems right.
