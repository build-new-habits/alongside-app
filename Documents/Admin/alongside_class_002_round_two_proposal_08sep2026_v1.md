# Steady Round — round two

**Proposal, 08 Sep 2026 — for Graeme to review**

The only piece of unfinished writing in the seven classes. Section 8, "Round two", is a **five-minute slot holding one beat and nine seconds of content**. The five movements were never written out, so the player would dutifully fill four minutes fifty with silence.

---

## What the class already decided

I have not invented a direction. Round two's own note says it:

> `[VOICE]` Same five, same order. I'll say less this time — you know what they are now.
>
> `⏸` *then the five, cued by name only, with the same holds*

And the reasoning, recorded when the class was written:

> **Round two is quieter on purpose.** Repeating the full coaching would say the person had not learned it; naming the movement and going quiet says they had.

So: the same five, in the same order, named and then silence.

---

## 🔴 The one judgement I made, and it goes against "name only"

**I kept the stop cues.** Shortened to their shortest form, but kept.

*"Cued by name only"* would drop them. I think that is wrong, for a reason the class itself supplies: **round two is when somebody is more tired.** The stop cues are the only safety-relevant lines in the class — *stop when it stops feeling smooth · back stays on the floor · slow beats far · come down before it gets ugly · while your back stays long* — and the round where form goes is the round where they matter most.

Coaching gets quieter. The limits do not.

If you disagree, deleting five short lines is a two-minute edit and the timing still fits.

---

## The proposal

Nine beats, replacing the single placeholder. **297 seconds of content in the 300-second slot** — it fits with three seconds spare, and no other section moves.

| | Line | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Same five, same order. I'll say less this time — you know what they are now.* | 10s |
| 2 | **Glute bridge.** | 40s |
| 3 | *Stop when it stops feeling smooth.* | 15s |
| 4 | **Dead bug.** Back stays on the floor. | 45s |
| 5 | **Bird dog.** Slow beats far. | 45s |
| 6 | **Plank.** Knees down is still a plank. | 10s |
| 7 | *Come down before it gets ugly.* | **held 35s** |
| 8 | **Hip hinge.** Stand up when you're ready. | 40s |
| 9 | *Only while your back stays long.* | 15s |
| 10 | *That's the five.* | 5s |

---

## Notes on specific choices

**"Glute bridge." on its own.** The first movement gets no qualifier at all — it is the plainest possible version of *you know what this is*. Everything after it carries one short line, because the other four have a limit worth restating and the bridge's cue arrives as beat 3 once they are moving.

**"Knees down is still a plank."** Round one says *"knees on the floor is a real version of this, not a lesser one."* Round two is exactly when somebody drops to their knees and feels they have failed, so the shortest form of that permission is worth its four words.

🔴 **The plank keeps `holdSeconds: 35`, identical to round one.** Not shortened for tiredness and not lengthened for progress. Round two is the same class, and a hold that changed between rounds would be the app deciding something about the person's capacity — which is the line this product does not cross.

**"That's the five."** Not "well done", not "that's it". Steady Round's leaving section already does the ending; this is a full stop, so the person knows the round is over rather than wondering whether another movement is coming.

---

## What it does not do

**No count of rounds completed, and no "one more than last time".** Steady Round's whole argument is *"I'd rather you did one round for six weeks than two rounds once."* A round-two that congratulated itself would undercut the exit offered two minutes earlier.

**No new movements.** Same five. A sixth would make round two a different thing, and the class card promises the same five twice.

---

## Open question

**Should round two acknowledge the person came back?** At present it does not — it begins as if continuing, which is the quietest reading. There is an argument for one line at the start that notices they carried on past the exit, and an argument that noticing it makes the exit feel like something they declined.

I have left it out. It is the one place I could see the class wanting a sentence I have not written.
