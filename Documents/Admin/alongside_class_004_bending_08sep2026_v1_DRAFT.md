## What this class is for

A back that copes is not a back that never hurts. It is a back that bends, and carries things, and gets a fright occasionally, and settles down again.

The strand is called *back-resilience* and almost everybody arriving at it has been taught the opposite lesson: that the back is fragile, that bending is dangerous, that the right response to a twinge is to stop moving. **This class is fifteen minutes of bending on purpose, slowly, with nothing loaded, so that bending stops being the thing you brace against.**

⚫ **It does not promise the back will stop hurting.** That is the promise everybody with a bad back has already been made and had broken. It offers something smaller and true: that the back moves, that you moved it deliberately, and that you chose where it stopped.

## The script

`[SCREEN]` is what appears; `[VOICE]` is what is said. `⏸` is silence, held.

---

### 1 · Before we start (1 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **We're going to bend, on purpose, slowly.**
Nothing heavy. Nothing fast. You choose where each one stops.

`[VOICE]` This one is about bending. Which, if your back has been a problem, might be the exact thing you've been avoiding.

`⏸ 10s`

`[VOICE]` Nothing here is loaded and nothing here is fast. Every movement has a point where you decide it stops, and I'll tell you what to listen for each time.

`⏸ 12s`

`[VOICE]` If your back is genuinely angry today, this isn't the day for it. Come back tomorrow — the class doesn't go anywhere.

`⏸ 12s`

🔴 **The exit is offered before the first movement, not after somebody has already hurt themselves finding out.** For this strand it has to come first: the population arriving here includes people who will push through and regret it, and the coach saying it out loud is what makes leaving an ordinary thing rather than a failure.

---

### 2 · Pelvic tilt (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Pelvic tilt** — on your back, knees bent. Roll the pelvis, small.
Stop while it still feels like nothing much.

`[VOICE]` On your back, knees bent, feet flat. Put a hand on your stomach if that helps you feel this.

`⏸ 15s`

`[VOICE]` Flatten your lower back gently towards the floor, then let it come back. That's the whole movement. It's smaller than you think it should be.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` Stop while it still feels like nothing much. This one isn't supposed to feel like anything — it's the back learning it can move a bit without a fuss.

`⏸ 35s`

**Exercise:** `pelvic-tilt` · **sit-out:** yes · **stop cue:** while it still feels like nothing much

---

### 3 · Cat-Cow (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Cat-Cow** — hands and knees, or seated.
As far as it goes today, which moves about.

`[VOICE]` Come onto your hands and knees, or stay in a chair and do the same thing sitting up.

`⏸ 15s`

`[VOICE]` Round your back up, then let it drop the other way. Slowly, and only as far as it goes today.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` How far that is changes day to day, and it isn't a score. Some days it's most of the way and some days it's barely anything, and both of those are just today.

`⏸ 30s`

**Exercise:** `cat-cow` · **sit-out:** yes · **seated route:** `thoracic-rotation-seated` · **stop cue:** as far as it goes today

---

### 4 · What that was like (1 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Did anything happen?**
Probably not. That's the useful bit.

`[VOICE]` Come back to lying, or sitting.

`⏸ 15s`

`[VOICE]` Did anything happen? Most likely not much. That's not a disappointing answer — for this, it's the whole answer.

`⏸ 25s`

⚫ **A `reflect` beat, and the question is deliberately low. "Did anything happen"** is answerable by somebody who felt nothing, and *nothing happened* is the outcome this class is actually gathering evidence for. Asking "how does your back feel?" invites a survey of every ache, which is the opposite exercise.

---

### 5 · Glute bridge (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Glute bridge** — lift the hips, hold a second, down slowly.
Stop when it stops feeling smooth.

`[VOICE]` Still on your back, knees bent. Push through your heels and let your hips come up. Not high. Down slowly.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` This is the back and the hips working together, which is mostly what a back that copes is.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` Stop when it stops feeling smooth — same signal as always, and it's still the useful one.

`⏸ 30s`

**Exercise:** `glute-bridge` · **sit-out:** yes · **stop cue:** when it stops feeling smooth

---

### 6 · Hip hinge (3 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Hip hinge** — standing, or seated. Hips back, chest forward.
Only while your back stays long.

`[VOICE]` Stand up if that's available. Take your time getting there — no rush, and no prize for speed.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Feet about hip width. Push your hips backwards and let your chest come towards the floor. Your back stays long the whole way — you're bending at the hips, not curling the spine.

`⏸ 35s`

`[VOICE]` If standing isn't happening, do it sitting: hinge forward from the hips with a long back. Same movement, same lesson, and not a lesser one.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` The moment your back starts to round, that's your range today. It moves about. That's normal, and it's not a warning.

`⏸ 35s`

**Exercise:** `hip-hinge-drill` · **sit-out:** yes · **seated route:** `seated-hip-hinge` · **stop cue:** while your back stays long

🔴 **The hinge is the point of the class.** Everything before it is preparation and everything after it is settling. It is placed sixth deliberately: one transition to standing, at the point where getting up is a change rather than a demand — the same reasoning as Steady Round's fifth.

---

### 7 · Settling (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Nothing to do.**
Knees to one side if that's comfortable.

`[VOICE]` Back down to the floor, or back into the chair. Let your knees fall to one side if that feels good, or don't.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` You bent your back about forty times in the last ten minutes. Deliberately, and slowly, and you chose where each one stopped.

`⏸ 45s`

`[VOICE]` Whether it helps is not a today question. It's a few-weeks question.

`⏸ 40s`

🔴 **"Whether it helps is not a today question"** is the same honesty as Class 003's *later in the week — not now*, and it is here for the same reason. The alternative — *this will strengthen your back* — is a claim the class cannot verify and this person has been promised before by somebody who was wrong.

---

### 8 · Leaving (1 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Done.** Take your time getting up.

`[VOICE]` Roll onto one side and come up from there when you're ready.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` That's it. Fifteen minutes of bending, and nothing went wrong.

`⏸ 15s`

`[VOICE]` See you next time.

## The lighter variant

**Omits sections 5 and 6** — the glute bridge and the hinge.

What is left is the tilt, the cat-cow, the noticing, the settling and the leaving: **the bending, without the strength work.** Ten minutes exactly.

⚠️ **The sections were reshaped so that it lands on ten.** They first came to 10.5, which the card rounds up to *"about 15 minutes"* — the same label as the full class, so nothing a person read could tell the two apart. The hinge went to 3 minutes and the settling to 2. **Both changes are defensible on their own terms** — the hinge is the point of the class and had the least time on it — but the reason they were made was arithmetic, and that is worth knowing when you judge them.

⚫ **The two that come out are the two that load the back.** On a day that is not great, the movements to keep are the ones that ask the back to move; the ones to drop are the ones that ask it to work. That distinction is the whole class in miniature, which is why the lighter version is still recognisably this class and not a different, blander one.

`[VOICE]` on a lighter day, replacing section 5: *Shorter one today. Same idea — we're bending, not lifting.*

## What this class did to the contract

**Nothing broke.** Third class in a row to say so, which is now worth something.

It is the first class written WITH a lighter variant rather than having one fitted afterwards, and that changed how it was written: sections 5 and 6 were built to be removable, which is why the hinge sits at 6 and not at 3. **A class written knowing it has a lighter version puts its load-bearing work where it can be taken out.**

Two things it did not need and deliberately did not add:

**No `intensityBias` beyond `gentle`.** There was a pull towards marking the hinge as harder than the rest. It is, but the class already handles that with a stop cue and a seated route, and a per-beat intensity field would be a fourth way of saying "this one might be too much" alongside `sitOut`, `stopCue` and the routes.

**No field for "come back tomorrow".** Section 1 offers leaving in the voice, which is where it belongs. Making it structural would turn a sentence the coach says into a decision the app makes.

## Open questions for Graeme

1. **Is "Bending" the right name?** It is the honest one and it may be the frightening one. "Bending" says exactly what the class is for; it also says the word some people have been avoiding for years. That may be the point or may be a barrier on the card, before anybody has heard the reassurance inside.

2. **Forty bends.** Section 7 says *"about forty times"*. The real count depends on how many the person did, and the contract has no way to know. Either it becomes vaguer — *"a lot of times"* — or it stays a confident number that will sometimes be wrong.

3. **The lighter variant's replacement line.** One sentence is proposed. Steady Round needed none because the class already made the case in its own voice. This one may not need one either.
