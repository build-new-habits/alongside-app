## What this class is for

🔴 **This is the strand most likely to be a session, not a class**, and it has to earn the difference. The builder already does leg strength perfectly well: sets, reps, progression. If this class is a worse version of that, it should not exist.

What it adds is what the twelve aims are actually about. Read them:

> Get off the floor without using my hands · Get down on the floor and play, and get back up · Get up the stairs without stopping halfway · Carry the shopping without my back going · Pick up somebody I love without worrying about it · Keep walking as far as I do now · Feel steadier on my feet · Keep doing things for myself

**Ten of the twelve are things people do, not numbers they want to hit.** Only two — *lift heavier*, *get faster* — are performance. So this class is not leg day. **It is four things you already do today, done on purpose, named for what they are for.**

⚫ **It never says "functional".** The word belongs to the people who write about this, not the people doing it. The class says *this is the one that is stairs*, and lets that be the whole explanation.

## The script

`[SCREEN]` is what appears; `[VOICE]` is what is said. `⏸` is silence, held.

---

### 1 · Before we start (1 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Four things you already do.**
A chair, and a wall or a bottom step. That's it.

`[VOICE]` You'll want a chair for this one, and a wall or the bottom step of the stairs.

`⏸ 10s`

`[VOICE]` Four movements, and you already do all four of them. Getting out of a chair. Going up a step. Going up on your toes. Holding still against a wall.

`⏸ 12s`

`[VOICE]` We're doing them on purpose, that's all. Nothing here is a new skill.

`⏸ 15s`

⚫ **"You already do all four of them" is the load-bearing sentence in the opening**, and it is the reason this class is not a session. Legs get trained; **this names what the training is already for**, which is the thing somebody who is tired of exercising needs to hear first.

---

### 2 · Getting out of a chair (2 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Sit to stand** — no hands if you can, hands if you need them.
Stop while the last one still looks like the first.

`[VOICE]` Sit towards the front of the chair, feet flat. Stand up, and sit back down. Slowly on the way down — that's the half most people give away.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Hands on your knees is completely fine and it isn't cheating. Most people who can do this without hands got there by doing it with hands first.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` Keep going for a bit. And here's your stopping point: stop while the last one still looks like the first one did.

`⏸ 40s`

`[VOICE]` This is the one that's getting off the sofa, and out of the car, and off the floor eventually.

`⏸ 25s`

**Exercise:** `sit-to-stand` · **sit-out:** yes · **stop cue:** while the last one still looks like the first

🔴 **"Stop while the last one still looks like the first"** is the stop cue for the whole class and it is doing the same job as Steady Round's *smoothness, not failure*. It is observable, it happens before anything goes wrong, and **it does not require the person to judge their own effort** — which is the judgement most likely to be wrong in both directions.

---

### 3 · What that was like (1 min)

`[SCREEN]` **How many was that?**
You don't have to know. Nobody was counting.

`[VOICE]` How many was that? You don't have to know — I wasn't counting and neither was the app.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` If you found yourself counting anyway, that's worth noticing. Most of us were taught to.

`⏸ 20s`

⚫ **A `reflect` beat that undoes the counting rather than gathering it.** In a strength class the expected question is *how many did you manage*, and asking it would turn the session into a score. This asks the opposite question and treats the impulse to count as something to notice, not something to correct.

---

### 4 · Going up a step (2 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Step-up** — bottom step, or a low step, or the kerb outside.
Lead with the same leg for a bit, then swap.

`[VOICE]` Find your step. The bottom stair is ideal. Low is better than high — this isn't about how big the step is.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Step up with one leg, bring the other to meet it, and come back down. Hold the bannister or the wall. Everybody holds something.

`⏸ 35s`

`[VOICE]` Same leg leading for a while, then swap. You'll have a side that's easier. That's normal and it's information, not a fault.

`⏸ 40s`

`[VOICE]` This one is stairs. That's the whole reason it's here.

`⏸ 25s`

**Exercise:** `step-up-glute-focus` · **sit-out:** yes · **stop cue:** while the last one still looks like the first

---

### 5 · Going up on your toes (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Calf raise** — standing at the chair, or seated.
Slow up, slower down.

`[VOICE]` Stand behind the chair and hold the back of it. Come up onto your toes, and down slowly.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` If standing isn't the thing today, do it sitting down — same movement, and it still counts.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` Slow on the way down. That's where this one actually happens.

`⏸ 35s`

**Exercise:** `chair-supported-calf-raise` · **sit-out:** yes · **seated route:** `seated-calf-raise` · **stop cue:** while the last one still looks like the first

---

### 6 · Holding still (2 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Wall sit** — as low as is comfortable, which may not be very low.
Come out of it while it's still steady.

`[VOICE]` Back against the wall, and slide down as far as is comfortable. That might be barely at all, and barely at all is a real wall sit.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Hold it while it's steady. Come out before the shaking starts — the shaking isn't the good bit, it's the exercise finishing without you noticing.

`⏸ 30s` **← held: 30s**

`[VOICE]` Stand up when you're ready.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` That's the one that's carrying something heavy while you find your keys.

`⏸ 25s`

**Exercise:** `isometric-wall-sit` · **sit-out:** yes · **hold:** 30s · **stop cue:** while it's still steady

🔴 **The 30 seconds is a `holdSeconds` and must never scale with pacing.** Same rule as Steady Round's plank, second instance. At 1.5x it becomes 20 seconds — a different exercise — and at 0.5x it becomes 60, which is the opposite of what somebody choosing *slower* was asking for.

---

### 7 · Settling (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Done.**
Four things you already do, done on purpose.

`[VOICE]` Sit down. Let your legs be heavy — they've earned it.

`⏸ 40s`

`[VOICE]` Four movements, and every one of them was something you already do in a normal week. Now they've had some attention.

`⏸ 45s`

`[VOICE]` Legs get better slowly and quietly. You'll notice it on a staircase in a month, not in this chair now.

`⏸ 30s`

🔴 **"You'll notice it on a staircase in a month, not in this chair now"** is the same honesty as Class 003's *later in the week — not now* and Class 004's *not a today question*. Third instance across five classes, which makes it **a pattern in the voice rather than a line**: the class never claims a result it cannot show you.

**Lighter variant line:** *Two movements, and both of them are things you already do in a normal week. Now they've had some attention.*

---

### 8 · Leaving (1 min)

`[SCREEN]` **See you next time.**

`[VOICE]` That's it. Nothing dramatic, which is rather the point with legs.

`⏸ 15s`

`[VOICE]` See you next time.

## The lighter variant

**Omits sections 4 and 6** — the step-up and the wall sit. Ten minutes.

What is left is getting out of the chair, the noticing, the calf raise, the settling and the leaving.

⚫ **The two that come out are the two that need something other than the chair** — a step, and a wall to slide down — **and they are also the two that load hardest.** What remains can be done sitting in one place, and one of the two remaining movements has a seated route. **On a bad day the class becomes something you can do without going anywhere**, which is a different quality from simply being shorter.

Section 7's line changes, because *four movements* is not true on a lighter day, and the count is the sentence's whole point.

---

## What this class did to the contract

**Nothing broke.** Fourth in a row.

It is the second class to use `holdSeconds`, which matters more than the first: **one instance of a field is a special case and two is a pattern.** The wall sit and the plank are unrelated movements in unrelated classes, and both need a duration that a pacing control must not touch.

It is also the second to need a `lighterVoice`, and for the same reason as Ground and Stopping Early — **a line that counts something the lighter variant changes.** Three of three so far. That is worth noticing: **if the count is in the voice, the lighter variant will contradict it.** A future class might be better written to avoid counting out loud at all, and that is a voice decision rather than a contract one.

## Open questions for Graeme

1. **"Standing Up" as a name.** It is the first movement, it is what the class is for, and it quietly means the other thing — staying able to do things for yourself. That last part may be exactly right or may be heavier than a class card should carry.

2. **The wall sit needs a wall, and the step needs a step.** Every other class so far needs nothing. This is the first with a real environmental requirement, and both are in the lighter variant's omit list — which means **a lighter day is also the version for somebody who has neither.** That is convenient, and it may be too convenient: it conflates *bad day* with *small flat*.

3. **"Carrying something heavy while you find your keys"** — the wall sit's reason. It is the most specific image in any class so far. Specific is usually good and occasionally it is somebody else's life.
