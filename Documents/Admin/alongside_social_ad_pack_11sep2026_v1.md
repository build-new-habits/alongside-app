## 4. Film scope, and one recommendation against the brief

Five films at 30 seconds. Each is one idea. A film that covers everything gets remembered for nothing.

| # | Film | Idea | Tier shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | Meet Alongside | The whole product in 30 seconds, free then the Plan | Both |
| **F2** | It asks first | The check-in, end to end | Free |
| **F3** | If it hurts | The exercise card, and what it tells you before you start | Free |
| **F4** | Four ways in | The Plan as a club, not a stricter coach | Plan |
| **F5** | Say where you are going | Naming a destination changes what the coach can say | Plan |

**Held back: "Built for the harder days."** It is the most persuasive film available and it cannot be recorded yet. See §9.

### 🔴 The speed-up, reconsidered

Graeme's brief proposes speeding up scenes to look like zooming between screens. **I would use it once, in F1, and nowhere else.**

Fast motion between screens is the visual grammar of every fitness advert, and this product's argument is that it is not one of those. It also costs the audience: motion sensitivity and vestibular discomfort are common in exactly the group being addressed, and WCAG 2.2 asks that motion be avoidable rather than decorative. A single speed ramp in the first four seconds reads as a deliberate contrast. Five films of it reads as the thing we say we are not.

**Where the time actually goes:** cut the dead frames, not the real ones. Every screen in the live app has a settling animation of roughly a second before its content is usable. Trimming those is invisible to the viewer and buys back four or five seconds a film.

## 6. Production rules

- **Record on the device, not the emulator.** iPhone screen recording at 60fps, then cut to 30fps. The emulator renders type slightly differently and somebody will notice.
- **Dev panel.** `DEV_PANEL_ENABLED` is hardcoded `true` at `settings.js:492`. Never record Settings, and never triple-tap a version number on camera.
- **Two accounts, seeded once.** One free, one on the Plan, both with a name that is not Graeme's and no real conditions data. A condition or a pain score visible in a frame is a special-category disclosure in public.
- **No notifications.** Do not disturb on, battery above 80%, a plausible time on the status bar.
- **Cut the settling, keep the pace.** Trim the render animation at the head of each screen. Do not speed up a screen a person is reading.
- **One take per screen, no reshoots of coach text.** The coach's lines vary by time of day and by check-in state. If a retake produces different copy, the caption has to change with it.

## 7. Accessibility, WCAG 2.2 AA

Applies to every asset, and to the post itself.

| Requirement | How it is met |
|---|---|
| 1.1.1 Non-text content | Alt text written in §3.3, pasted at upload on every platform |
| 1.2.2 Captions, prerecorded | Burned-in captions on every film, in the tables above, plus an uploaded caption file where the platform takes one |
| 1.2.3 / 1.2.5 Audio description | The voice track states what is on screen rather than commenting on it, so no separate description is needed |
| 1.4.3 Contrast | Every pair measured: text 14.5:1, secondary 10.3:1, accent 9.6:1 on the background. No text sits on an image |
| 1.4.12 Text spacing | Line height 1.3 and above throughout; no text set in a fixed-height box |
| 2.2.2 Pause, stop, hide | No auto-advancing carousel; the platform's own controls are the only motion |
| 2.3.1 Three flashes | Nothing flashes. The single speed ramp in F1 is a scroll, not a cut sequence |
| 2.3.3 Animation from interactions | The speed ramp appears once, in one film, for four seconds |

Captions are burned in **and** uploaded where possible. Burned-in captions survive autoplay with sound off, which is how most of this will be seen; uploaded captions are what a screen reader and the platform's own transcript use.

## 8. Claim boundaries

Checked against `alongside_intended_purpose_statement_03sep2026_v1.docx`. These are the lines that cannot be crossed, and the wording that keeps us the right side of them.

- **No health claims.** Nothing in this pack says the app assesses, monitors, treats, manages, improves or reduces anything about a person. The films describe activity, never outcome.
- **Conditions are named only as things we avoid aggravating.** No film mentions a named condition at all, which is the simplest way to hold this.
- **The burnout wording on the live website is a problem.** "Alongside detects the early signs" of burnout and "responds with restoration" describes monitoring and a response to a state. That is the kind of sentence the intended purpose statement exists to catch, and every ad drives traffic to it. Flagged in §9.
- **No testimonials.** The rotating quotes on the homepage read as user quotes and no beta tester has used the product. None of them appear in this pack, and none should appear in an advert until a real tester says a real thing and agrees in writing.
- **No prices.** Until Stripe is live.
- **18+ targeting on every platform.** Alongside: Move is for adults.

## 10. 🟠 PROPOSAL-3 — found while scoping this pack

Walking the live app on a free account: energy **2/10**, mood **3/10**, sleep 7 hours and poor. The check-in summarised it correctly as *"A harder day"*. The proposal screen then offered, as the suggested session: **Glute Focus, 30 to 40 minutes, 10 movements**, described as loading the posterior chain progressively.

It may be an artefact of a test account with no onboarding answers, and the two lighter options (Breathing, Short walk) were present underneath. But `_generateOptions()` in `coach-proposal.js` passes `energyScore` only to the **fallback** path, and it writes `todayIntensity` from the phase bias before building. If the phase bias overwrites what the check-in just established, that is the BUILD-5 shape again: a value read from a field the writer never set.

**Needs a reversal-proven gate that executes, not a source-text check.** It is also the exact moment the held-back film would have to record, which is why it is in this document rather than a separate one.
