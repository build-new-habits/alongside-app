# Alongside: Move — Class 003: Stopping Early
## 06 Sep 2026 v1

Build New Habits | Strand: **pacing**. Format: **mindfulness**. Intensity: **gentle**.

Third written class, chosen to serve a strand neither of the first two touched, and to be led by attention rather than movement.

---

## What this class is for

**Pacing is one skill: stopping while you still have something left.** Everything else about it is consequence.

It is also the hardest thing to teach, because the evidence arrives late. You stop early, nothing dramatic happens, and two days later you are not wiped out — and almost nobody connects the second thing to the first.

🔴 **So this class practises stopping early on purpose, in a setting where it costs nothing.** Three short pieces of movement, each ended by the person **before** they want to. The point is not the movement. The point is rehearsing the decision somewhere it is safe to get wrong.

⚫ **The class does not promise it works.** It says what pacing is for, once, plainly, and then gets out of the way. Somebody living with post-exertional malaise has been promised things before.

### Flags, on the card

> **What's in it:** sitting · three short pieces of gentle movement · stopping each one early on purpose · quiet
>
> **You'll be asked to stop before you want to.** That is the practice, not a restriction.

🔴 **"Stopping early on purpose" is flagged because it is genuinely uncomfortable**, and for a different reason than the other flags. Somebody who has spent years being told to push through will feel this as failing. **Naming it on the card means the discomfort is expected rather than a sign the class is going wrong.**

## The script

`⏸` is `speechSeconds` and scales. **There are no `holdSeconds` in this class** — nothing is held to a duration, because the whole point is that the person chooses the end.

---

### 1 · What this is (1 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Pacing is one skill: stopping while you still have something left.**
We're going to practise it three times, where it costs nothing.

`[VOICE]` Sit somewhere you can stay for about ten minutes.

`⏸ 10s`

`[VOICE]` Pacing gets described a lot of ways. Underneath it, there's one skill: stopping while you've still got something left.

`⏸ 12s`

`[VOICE]` It's hard to learn because the proof turns up late. You stop early, nothing much happens, and then two days later you're alright — and almost nobody joins those two things up.

`⏸ 15s`

`[VOICE]` So we're going to practise the stopping, three times, somewhere it doesn't cost you anything.

`⏸ 10s`

---

### 2 · First one: shoulders (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Roll your shoulders. Slowly.**
Stop when you notice you could keep going.

`[VOICE]` Roll your shoulders backwards. Slowly, and only as big as is comfortable.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Here's the instruction, and it's an odd one. **Stop when you notice you could keep going.** Not when you're tired. When you first notice there's more in there.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` Whenever that is. There's no number.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` If you overshot and stopped later than that — completely normal. It's the first go.

`⏸ 12s`

**Exercise:** `neck-mobility` (shoulder portion) · **stop cue:** noticing there is more available

---

### 3 · What that was like (1 min)

`[SCREEN]` **How did stopping feel?**
Fine, annoying, pointless — all real answers.

`[VOICE]` How was that? Not the shoulders. The stopping.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Some people find it a relief. Some find it irritating, like being interrupted. Some think it's a bit pointless. All of those are the right answer.

`⏸ 20s`

⚫ **"Irritating" and "pointless" are named before the person has to.** If the only offered reactions are positive, somebody having a negative one concludes they are doing the class wrong — and the honest answer here is often irritation.

**Practice reference:** `mindful-observation`, turned inward on a decision rather than an object.

---

### 4 · Second one: reaching (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Reach one arm up. Then the other.**
Same instruction — stop while there's more.

`[VOICE]` Reach one arm up above your head. Only as far as it goes easily.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Down. Other arm.

`⏸ 20s`

`[VOICE]` Keep swapping. And stop while there's still more in it.

`⏸ 30s`

`[VOICE]` This one's usually easier than the shoulders. Second go at anything usually is.

`⏸ 15s`

**Exercise:** `thoracic-rotation-seated` (reach variant) · **stop cue:** same

---

### 5 · Third one: standing, if that's available (2 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Stand and sit, a few times. Slowly.**
Or stay seated and lift your heels — same practice.

`[VOICE]` If standing up and sitting down is available today, do that a few times. Slowly. If it isn't, stay where you are and lift your heels off the floor instead — that's the same practice and it isn't a lesser version.

`⏸ 25s`

`[VOICE]` Same instruction. Stop while there's more.

`⏸ 35s`

`[VOICE]` This is the one where stopping early is hardest, because it feels like so little.

`⏸ 20s`

**Exercise:** `sit-to-stand` (or seated heel raise) · **easier route:** seated heel raise · **stop cue:** same

---

### 6 · The quiet (2 min 30)

`[SCREEN]` **Nothing to do.**
You stopped three times and nothing bad happened.

`[VOICE]` Sit for a couple of minutes. Nothing to do.

`⏸ 40s`

`[VOICE]` You stopped three times today when you didn't have to. Whether that turns out to be useful, you'll find out later in the week — not now.

`⏸ 50s`

`⏸ 40s`

🔴 **"You'll find out later in the week — not now" is the honest form of the promise.** The alternative — *this is how you protect your energy* — is a claim the class cannot verify and the person has heard before. **Naming the delay is what makes the delay survivable.**

---

### 7 · Leaving (1 min)

`[SCREEN]` **Done.**

`[VOICE]` That's it. Twelve minutes, and the useful part was the three times you stopped.

`⏸ 10s`

`[VOICE]` See you next time.

## What this class did to the contract

**It held, with one addition and one confirmation.**

```
holdSeconds     — ABSENT throughout this class, and correctly so.
                  Nothing is held to a duration; the person chooses
                  every ending. Optional, not required.

stopCue         — confirmed general. Used identically three times, and
                  it is the entire pedagogy here rather than a safety
                  note. Class 002 discovered the field; this one shows
                  it carries teaching as well as caution.

reflectBeat     — NEW. A beat that asks a question and offers no
                  movement. Class 001 had two of these and I recorded
                  them as ordinary beats. They are not: they take no
                  exercise, no hold, and no stop cue, and the screen
                  and voice diverge more than anywhere else.
```

⚫ **`easierRouteId` earned its separateness again.** Seated heel raise is not a seated *version* of sit-to-stand — it is a different movement doing the same job. **That is a third distinct thing** from "same movement, less of it" and "same class, seated". Not adding a field for it yet: three classes is not enough to know whether it recurs.

## Open

🟡 **The 2m30 quiet is the longest silence in any class so far and is a guess.** In a class about stopping, sitting still afterwards is thematically right and may still be too long.

🟡 **The contract can be fixed now.** Three classes, three formats, four strands, and the shape has taken a circuit, a floor stretch and a seated mindfulness class without bending. **Fix it against these three before writing a fourth.**
