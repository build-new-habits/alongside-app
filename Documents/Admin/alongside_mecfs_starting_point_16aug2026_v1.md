## 1. The problem in one paragraph

The physiotherapist's review, verbatim:

> *"ME/CFS: Must never route through a standard load/pain adaptation model. Post-Exertional Malaise (PEM) is not mechanical load intolerance; it is neuro-immune. Adapting volume to 'how you feel today' is the exact mechanism that triggers severe baseline crashes. ME/CFS requires specialized pacing models (HR monitoring, energy envelopes), not progressive rehab."*

**"Adapting volume to how you feel today" is not a feature of Alongside. It is what Alongside is.** The check-in, the coach's proposal, the difficulty ceiling, Recovery Mode — all of it reads today and adjusts. So this is not a gap to patch. For this group, the core mechanic is the contraindication.

And it is uncomfortable, because people with ME/CFS are close to the centre of who this product exists for: failed by mainstream fitness, told to push through, and harmed by it. **The honest position is that being built for people mainstream fitness failed does not mean being built for everyone it failed.**

---

## 2. The thing I think has to be decided first

**`chronic-fatigue` is one condition ID covering two different populations.** It is labelled *"Chronic fatigue / ME-CFS"*, and those are not the same thing.

- Somebody persistently exhausted — from a thyroid condition, long-term stress, poor sleep, recovery from illness — is a person the adaptive model probably serves well.
- Somebody with ME/CFS and post-exertional malaise is a person the adaptive model may actively harm.

**One ID means any decision is wrong for one of the two groups.** Exclude, and we turn away people we could help. Allow, and we keep harming the people the review is about.

Three ways out, in rough order of my confidence:

| | Approach | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Split into two conditions** at onboarding: "Chronic fatigue" and "ME / CFS" | Cleanest. Asks people to self-identify with a diagnosis they may not have formally received |
| **B** | **Keep one ID, add one distinguishing question** about delayed crashes after activity | Catches the mechanism rather than the label, so it works for the undiagnosed. **But it is a clinical screening question and needs the physio to write it** |
| **C** | Treat the whole ID as ME/CFS | Safe, and wrong for a large group |

**B is what I would argue for, and it is not mine to write.** PEM is defined by the delayed crash, and asking about the mechanism catches people who have never been given a name for it — which, for this condition, is a lot of people.

## 3. What remains available — the real design question

Once somebody is identified, what does the app actually do? **Not "do we lock them out" — we do not lock anybody out. The question is what we honestly offer.**

| | Option | My read |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Everything stays, with a warning | **Weakest.** Informed and still served the contraindicated thing. This is a disclaimer protecting us, not a decision protecting them |
| **2** | **Non-exertional practices only** — breathing, mindfulness, seated practices. No sessions, no programmes, no difficulty ceiling | Honest and shippable. **Requires the physio to confirm what counts as non-exertional** — I will not classify that |
| **3** | Advise against use entirely, restrict nothing, leave it to them | Respects autonomy. Risks being a shrug |
| **4** | Build the pacing model — energy envelope, heart-rate ceiling, no progression | **The right answer, and its own stream.** Months, not days, and it needs clinical partnership rather than a review |

**My recommendation: 2 now, 4 as a named stream, and say so out loud in the copy.** Option 2 with the honesty of option 3 in the wording.

**One flag on option 2:** the severe-pain override already serves breathing, mindfulness and a mindful walk as its Gentle Care card. **A mindful walk is exertion.** If option 2 is chosen, that card almost certainly needs a variant for this group — and that is a question for the physio, not a judgement I should make.

## 4. Draft copy — three registers to react against

All three follow the same rule, which I would hold whatever the wording ends up being: **the failure belongs to the product, never to the person.** No "you can't". Only "I'm not built for this yet". Anything else reads as *you are too broken for this app*, and that is the precise harm this product exists to avoid.

### Draft A — plain and direct *(my recommendation)*

> "Thank you for telling me. I want to be straight with you about something.
>
> The way I work is to look at how you are feeling today and adjust what I suggest from there. For ME and CFS, the guidance I have been given is clear that this can do harm — because a good day can be followed by a crash two days later, and adjusting to today does not see that coming.
>
> So I am not going to pretend I am built for this yet. What I can offer you is the breathing and mindfulness work, which asks nothing of your energy. What I cannot offer is a plan that paces properly around your limits, and that is the thing that would actually help.
>
> I would rather tell you that than sell you something that sets you back."

### Draft B — shorter, less explanation

> "Thank you for telling me. I should be honest: I am not built for ME or CFS yet.
>
> I work by adjusting to how you feel today, and for this that can cause a crash a couple of days later. Pacing properly is a different job and I am not able to do it yet.
>
> The breathing and mindfulness work is still here, and it asks nothing of your energy."

### Draft C — leads with what is still there

> "The breathing and mindfulness work is here for you whenever you want it, and it asks nothing of your energy.
>
> What I am not going to do is build you sessions. I work by adjusting to how you feel today, and for ME and CFS that is the thing most likely to cause a crash later in the week. Until I can pace properly around your limits, I would rather do less and be honest about it."

**My note on the three:** A is the one I would ship. B is efficient but the compression makes it sound like a policy. C's instinct is right — lead with what remains rather than what does not — but it opens by offering something before acknowledging what the person just told us, and this product's rule is that the coach responds to the person before it offers anything.

## 5. Decisions that are yours

1. **Split, screening question, or treat all as ME/CFS** (§2) — and if a screening question, it goes to the physio to write.
2. **What remains available** (§3).
3. **Which register** (§4), and the final wording.
4. **Where this appears.** Onboarding is obvious. It must also fire when somebody adds the condition later at Conditions Update — **two entry points, or the whole thing is reachable only by new users**, which is a fault this codebase has shipped before.
5. **Does it repeat, or is it said once?** Said once risks being missed. Repeated becomes nagging about a condition somebody already knows they have. My instinct: once at declaration, then never again unless they ask.
6. **Do we offer to tell them when this changes?** Kit is set up and free to 10,000. An opt-in — *"I will let you know when I can do this properly"* — turns a refusal into a commitment. It also means promising something we then have to deliver.

## 6. Questions for the physiotherapist

Short follow-up, and it can go with the fibromyalgia and osteoporosis question already outstanding.

1. **Is a distinguishing question between chronic fatigue and ME/CFS appropriate for a non-clinical app** — and if so, what should it say? We think the delayed crash is the mechanism to ask about, but we will not write that question ourselves.
2. **What is genuinely non-exertional here?** Breathing and seated mindfulness, we assume. **A mindful walk we do not assume** — the app currently offers one as part of its lowest-intensity option.
3. **Is somebody with chronic fatigue but no post-exertional malaise safe with the normal adaptive model?**
4. **Is there anything in the drafts above that a clinician would object to** — particularly anything that could read as discouraging activity altogether, which we understand is its own harm.
5. **Fibromyalgia and osteoporosis** — both were named in our original questions and neither was addressed. Both are currently in the same state as hypermobility was: collected, then ignored by the exercise filter.
