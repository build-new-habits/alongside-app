# Alongside: Move — The Tier Boundary
## 12 Aug 2026 v2

Build New Habits | Why someone pays, what they feel, and how they find out. Sits beside `alongside_experience_blueprint_22jun2026_v1.md`. That document is the map of the park; this one is the answer to *what is the ticket for*.

Produced from a working conversation, 12 Aug 2026. Several positions in it were argued, tested and discarded before landing — the discarded ones are recorded, because knowing why an answer was rejected is what stops it being proposed again in six months.

---

## 1. The answer, in one line

> **Free gives you a coach for today. Personal gives you a coach who knows where you're going.**

---

## 2. Why people stay vs. why people pay — both load-bearing

**These are two different jobs and conflating them was the central error in this conversation.**

Three answers were proposed as *the reason someone converts* and rejected. **They are not rejected as concepts.** They are the reasons people stay, and staying is most of the business — retention and word of mouth are the only channel this product can afford. Recorded here so no future session deletes them as dead ends.

| | Why people **stay** — keep and build these | Why they **pay** |
|---|---|---|
| **Deepening relationship** | The coach that has known you a while genuinely feels different. It is why month six beats month one | ✗ Cannot carry the tier: 97% are gone before day 30. A promise paying out in month twelve is one almost nobody lives to collect |
| **Impact and the vote** | Real, distinctive, and worth feeling good about. Part of why someone tells a friend | ✗ Killed in one line: *"Why don't you just give them a pound? Then you've done a hundred percent."* At 5% of revenue it is a reason to feel good about paying, not a reason to pay |
| **Transfer outward** | The empathy arc — caring for yourself becomes caring for others. Named in the Experience Blueprint as one of three things nobody else does | ✗ Not as *"this will help you carry the shopping"* — patronising, done badly by every competitor, and it assumes the app knows what a person's life demands |

**The common fault in all three as conversion arguments:** they priced *feelings*. Feelings are why people stay and why they talk. **Capability is why they pay.**

## 2b. The rejected framings, specifically

**Wrong answer 1: "Premium is the coach that has known you for a year."**
Longitudinal depth as the paid tier. Killed by the market: **97% of users are gone before day 30.** A promise that pays out in month twelve is one almost nobody lives to collect, and it asks people to pay now for value that arrives later — the same deal every failing fitness app offers. Longitudinal depth is a *reward for staying*, never the reason to buy.

**Wrong answer 2: "Premium is where your money reaches beyond you."**
Impact credits and the community vote as the paid tier. Killed in one sentence by Graeme: *"Why don't you just give them a pound? Then you've done a hundred percent."* At 5% of revenue, allocation is a reason to feel good about paying. It is not a reason to pay. It cannot carry the tier.

**Wrong answer 3: "Premium is transfer to real life."**
Exercise pointed outward — *this will help you carry the shopping, get off the floor.* Rejected as patronising, and as the thing every competitor already does badly. Nobody's trumpet teacher ever said *"practise scales in case you need them one day."* It also assumes the app knows what a person's life demands, which is the opposite of this product's stance.

**The common fault in all three:** they priced *feelings*. Feelings are why people stay and why they talk. **Capability is why they pay.**

## 3. What a coach actually does

The question that unlocked it, from Graeme: *"What would a coach do?"*

Not remembering. Not encouragement. This:

> **A coach connects today's work to something you chose that isn't today.**

Scales are not practice. Scales are *so that when the run in bar 34 arrives, your fingers already know it*. Interval training is not a drill — it is *so the leap in the third movement doesn't frighten you*. Anna's athletics coach knows she wants to compete at English Schools, knows what the girls around her are running, and shapes every session toward March.

The transfer points **forward, to a destination the person named** — not outward to a life the app has guessed at. That distinction is dignity, and it is the same distinction the whole product is built on.

### The track analogy — the boundary in full

Graeme turns up at the track and asks Anna's coach for a session. The coach gives him a real one: properly judged, adapted to what he can do, genuinely worth having. He leaves satisfied.

**The coach is withholding nothing.** There is nothing to withhold, because Graeme has not named a destination. You cannot build a road to nowhere.

Anna has named one. So there is a plan, a sequence, an idea of where she needs to be by March, and it adapts every week.

**Free is the session. Personal is the plan.**

This is what makes the boundary honest and what makes it survive **Locked Principle P1** (*the coach never withholds what it can see*). The free coach is not hiding a plan. For someone who has not named a goal, the plan does not exist.

## 4. The boundary

### Free — an excellent session, every time

- A real session, adapted to today: energy, pain, capability, equipment, training intent
- Full body only. **The coach decides.** No session-type or duration selection
- Every safety feature, permanently. Care mode. Condition-aware exclusion
- The empathy transfer arc, grounding moments, **and In Step** — moved to free (12 Aug). In Step is a *practice*, not a journey; the paid act is naming where you are going, not access to content. This also resolves the awkward split where the empathy work straddled the paywall
- **A record**: fourteen days of what you did
- Lift notes and recall

Free is **complete in itself and limited in scope**. Nothing inside it is degraded to create pressure. What is missing is a destination, not quality.

### Personal — a coach who knows where you're going

- **You name what you're working toward.** The coach builds the road
- Every session arrives **with its reason attached** — why this, why today, why lighter than Saturday
- **The road moves when life does.** Ill for a week, flat for a fortnight, ahead of schedule — the plan adapts rather than leaving you behind
- **The coach teaches you to make the call yourself.** Why upper/lower, when to pull back. A deepening capacity, not just more buttons
- All session types and durations, with the reasoning
- **Progress that reads rather than records** — see §4.1
- Grounding moments woven to what you are actually doing
- Impact credits and the community vote

### 4.1 Progress must differ in KIND, not length

If free is fourteen days and Personal is ninety, we are selling a bigger number, and bigger numbers are easy to shrug at.

- **Free records.** *"That's four this fortnight."* What you did. True, useful, complete.
- **Personal reads.** *"You've come in low-energy eleven times. Nine of those, you finished. I don't think you know that about yourself."*

That is not more data. It is a different act — and it is only possible when there is a destination to read toward. Anna's coach would never say that to the drop-in, not because he is withholding it, but because a single session contains nothing to read.

**Governed by P4:** the coach points at what it noticed. It never attaches a verdict.

## 5. Onboarding — keep the question, change what follows

Onboarding asks what the person is hoping for. **This stays.** Removing it would break the product's first and most important moment, and a free user who is never asked never discovers they have a destination worth naming.

What changes is the response. The coach is honest:

> *"That's worth building toward properly — that's a plan, and I can't build you one yet. But I've got what you said, and it shapes what I put in front of you today."*

This is not a pitch. It is true: the answer genuinely informs session selection at both tiers. What differs is whether it becomes a **road**.

**Two rules.**

**Protect the person with no goal.** *"I don't know, I just want to move more"* is a real and complete answer. It is the drop-in at the track. Nothing in the copy may make it feel like a failed question.

**Goals are always named, never inferred.** *"I want to still be able to get up off the floor when I'm 80"* is a genuine destination and is Anna's English Schools in a different register. What must never happen is the app deducing a goal from someone's age or capability.

## 6. The door — always open, never pushed

### The correction that produced this section

An earlier draft placed the upgrade offer at moments of friction: after a goal is named, at four weeks, when a plan fails to adapt. Graeme rejected it, and was right:

> *"That's us deciding when the user is ready to receive it. It's us deciding when the friction is. I wouldn't wait two weeks — I might have already got bored and stopped using the app altogether. They'll only know what they know. They don't know what they don't know."*

Two faults. It **decides for the person** when they are ready, which is the paternalism this product exists to refuse. And it **risks the whole customer**: someone bored at day nine is gone before any trigger fires.

### The model: the cast member

Graeme's own Disney answer, from earlier in the same conversation — a cast member visibly dressed to be recognised as someone to ask, standing there permanently, never grabbing anyone.

> **The door is visible at all times. The person walks through it when they choose.**

**This does not breach P3.** P3 governs *interruptions* — teaching in the abstract, on a timer, unrequested. A permanent, non-interrupting surface is not an interruption. It is a door that is always there, and the person decides when to look at it.

**And it does not breach P1**, because the coach is not the one speaking. This is the **P2 helper layer**: visibly distinct, never in coach voice.

### What "always visible" means concretely

A persistent, quiet, unmissable surface on the screens where the difference is real:

- **Progress** — beneath the fourteen-day record: *"There's more in this than a fortnight can show. I can read the pattern instead of just listing it."* Visible from day one, not week two.
- **After onboarding, and permanently on Home** — *"Working toward something? I can build a plan and adapt it as you go."*
- **Session screen** — where the coach's one-line reason sits, a quiet marker that the reasoning goes deeper.

Never modal. Never mid-session. Never dismissible-then-gone, because it is not an interruption to be cleared — it is furniture.

### The nudge

A single prompt may still fire later — but its job is **not to introduce**. By then the person has seen the door for weeks. The nudge only says *now might be the moment*.

> *"You've been at this a month. Want me to start building toward something?"*

Once. Dismissible. Never repeated for the same thing. It cannot be a surprise, because nothing new is being revealed.

## 7. Language rules

**Never say "upgrade to Personal" in-product.** As Graeme put it: *"to you and me that's the tier — but 'that's personal' reads as 'you shouldn't ask me that.'"* The possessive collision is unavoidable in almost every sentence worth writing.

**Offer the capability, in plain English. Name the tier only where money changes hands.**

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Upgrade to Personal for goal setting" | "Working toward something? I can build a plan around it." |
| "Personal unlocks 90-day progress" | "There's more in this than a fortnight can show." |
| "Personal tier: all session types" | "Want to understand how to make this call yourself?" |

The word *Personal* belongs on the pricing page and the account screen. Everywhere else, describe the thing.

---

## 8. The pub test

The measure agreed for all of this: would someone, unprompted, over a glass of wine, tell a friend?

**Free earns the sentence.** *"My fitness app asked me to think about someone in my life who's having a hard time — and I did, and it stayed with me all week."* Nobody repeats a workout summary. People absolutely repeat that.

**Personal earns a different one.** *"I told it what I was aiming for and it built the whole thing round it. I was ill for a week and it just moved everything — it didn't make me feel like I'd fallen behind."*

The free tier makes the app **remarkable**. The premium tier makes it **theirs**.

---

## 9. Honest scoping — the plan does not exist yet

**This is the most important line in the document.**

`programmes.js` holds eight programmes and `programmeEngine.js` tracks weeks, phases and milestones. That is not the same as *"you name a destination, the coach builds a road toward it, explains each session's place on it, and moves it when life happens."*

That is a **real build**, not a wiring fix, and it is now the answer to why anyone pays. It should be scoped properly rather than assumed to be nearly there.

### Build sequence

| # | Item | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Condition-aware empathy selection** | Small. The prompts, stages and conditions already exist; only the matcher is missing. Turns the differentiator from present to felt. Nothing else improves free this cheaply |
| 2 | **The always-visible door** | Cheap, and it stops losing people at day nine. No point building the plan if nobody knows to ask |
| 3 | **Grounding moments** | The in-exercise layer. Frequent, light, unbound from the arc. This is what earns the pub sentence for free users |
| 4 | **The plan** | The paid spine. Largest piece. Needs its own specification before any code |
| 5 | **Progress that reads** | Depends on 4 — there is nothing to read toward without a destination |
| 6 | **Teach-you-to-decide** | The deepening capacity. Sits on top of 4 |

Items 1–3 are free-tier work and make the product remarkable. Items 4–6 are why someone pays.

---

## 10. Open questions

1. **What is a destination, concretely?** An event with a date, a capability, a frequency, something felt? The answer shapes the whole plan build.
2. **How does the road adapt?** What happens after two missed weeks — shift, rescale, or ask?
3. **Grounding moment content.** Graeme's plank and running examples are the model; the library needs writing.
4. **Free-tier session variety.** Full body only — does the coach still vary emphasis day to day, or is every free session structurally identical? Identical would be a poor session, not a limited one.
